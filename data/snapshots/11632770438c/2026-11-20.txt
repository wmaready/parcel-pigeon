For the reasons stated in the
preamble, the Department of Veterans
Affairs proposes to amend 38 CFR part
8 as set forth below:
PART 8—NATIONAL SERVICE LIFE
INSURANCE
■ 1. The authority citation for part 8
continues to read as follows:
Authority: 38 U.S.C. 501, 1901–1929,
1981–1988, unless otherwise noted.
■ 2. In § 8.2, add paragraph (f) to read
as follows:
§ 8.2 Payment of premiums.
* * * * *
(f) What effect does a paid-up
declaration have upon the payment of
premiums? Notwithstanding paragraphs
(a) through (e) of this section, no
premiums shall be due or payable for
any policy described in § 8.37 on and
after the effective date specified therein.
■ 3. Add the undesignated center
heading ‘‘Other Provisions’’
immediately before § 8.31.
■ 4. Add § 8.37 to read as follows:
§ 8.37 Paid-up status of National Service
Life Insurance.
(a) Paid-up declaration and effective
date. Effective [INSERT DATE 30 DAYS
AFTER DATE OF PUBLICATION OF
THE FINAL RULE IN THE Federal
Register], all National Service Life
Insurance policies issued under 38
U.S.C. 1904 with policy prefix V and
policies issued under 38 U.S.C. 1925
with policy prefixes J and JR that are not
paid-up, terminated, or in force under
the extended term insurance provision
(§ 8.14) are considered paid-up and no
further premiums are required to
maintain such policies in force. A
policy that is in force under extended
term insurance as of the paid-up
declaration effective date and is later
approved for reinstatement under § 8.7
shall be considered paid-up as of the
paid-up declaration effective date, and
no premiums shall be required to
maintain such policy in force for any
period beginning on or after that date.
(b) Continuation of policy provisions.
Upon entering into non-premium-
paying status following the paid-up
declaration under paragraph (a), such
policies shall remain in effect subject to
all contract provisions and applicable
regulations except for the premium-
payment requirements.
(c) Advance premiums. Any
premiums paid in advance but not yet
applied as of the paid-up declaration
effective date shall be held on deposit
on the policy of the insured to accrue
interest until the insured requests a
refund or until the policy matures. If the
insured requests a refund, or if the
policy matures before the insured
requests a refund, VA will remit the
advance premiums plus any accrued
interest.
(d) Effect of paid-up status on other
provisions. Notwithstanding any other
provision of this part, for any policy
described in § 8.37(a):
(1) No premiums may be billed,
collected, remitted, deducted, or
otherwise required under §§ 8.2 through
8.5 as of the paid-up declaration date.
(2) Any lien or policy loan existing on
a policy as of the paid-up declaration
date shall remain in effect and shall
continue to be subject to the terms of the
policy and to §§ 8.11 through 8.15, as
applicable. Nothing in this section shall
be construed to cancel, forgive, or
otherwise alter any such indebtedness
or lien.
(3) Dividends shall be held on deposit
on the policy of the insured to accrue
interest in accordance with § 8.10(g),
unless the insured elects another option
under § 8.10(e)–(g).
(4) Any pre-authorized debit,
allotment, or deduction authorization
related to premium payment shall
automatically terminate on the paid-up
declaration effective date.

II. Proposal
Background. The Postal Service
contends that PTR should replace
ODIS–RPW because PTR provides more
complete data, considering the ODIS–
RPW sample reduction approved in
Commission Order No. 9449 took effect
in FY 2026, Quarter 4.5 The Postal
Service notes that, in Docket No.
RM2021–3, it identified PTR as a
potential alternative source for
determining the percentages of mail
transported by air ‘‘to and from the
offshore location.’’ 6 Following approval
of the changes proposed in that docket,
the Postal Service now considers it
appropriate to use PTR for the relevant
portions of the Priority Mail
transportation model.’’ Petition,
Proposal at 4.
The current model uses ODIS–RPW
sample data for two principal
purposes.7 First, it distributes Alaska,
Hawaii, and Air Taxi costs by zone.
Second, it estimates the portions of
Priority Mail involving ground-air or
air-ground connections and the portion
of Zone 1 mail classified as Intra-SCF.
Petition, Proposal at 2. The Postal
Service states that the current model
pulls ODIS–RPW data from the origin-
destination ZIP (ODZIP) file which
identifies the origin-destination ZIP3
pairs in the sample and reports the
corresponding Priority Mail weight and
volume for the fiscal year. Id. Although
the Postal Service considers the existing
sample large, it states that reducing the
sample size will increase statistical
variance. Id.
Proposal and rationale. The Postal
Service proposes replacing the ODIS–
RPW-based ODZIP dataset with a more
complete PTR dataset because PTR
covers the relevant population-level
census data, rather than a statistical
sample, thereby eliminating sampling
variance in the reported weights and
volumes. Id. at 3. The Postal Service
asserts that PTR data will be more
representative of all Priority Mail,
providing mail piece data for more
ODZIP3 pairs. Id.
Impact. The Postal Service asserts that
it sees changes in the weights by zone
for Alaska, Hawaii, and Air Taxi
networks because they rely on inputs
from the ODIS–RPW sample data. Id. at
4. For highway-related inputs to the
Transportation Cost Model workbook,
the Postal Service reports a 0.87-
percentage-point increase in the share of
distance-related ground transportation
costs associated with ground-to-air or
air-to-ground connections. Id. It also
reports a 7.41-percentage-point increase
in the share of Local and Zone 1 weight
attributable to Intra-SCF mail.8 The
Postal Service asserts that ‘‘[t]hese shifts
are a result of the PTR data having more
complete coverage of all ZIP3
combinations relative to the ODIS–RPW
sample data.’’ Petition, Proposal at 4.
The Postal Service concludes that
replacing ODIS–RPW sample data with
census data from PTR results in an
improvement in the quality, accuracy,
and completeness of the Priority Mail
transportation model and has minimal
impact on the final unit cost by zone. Id.
at 5.
