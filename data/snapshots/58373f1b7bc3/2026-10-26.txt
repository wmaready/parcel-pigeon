POSTAL SERVICE
39 CFR Part 111
New Mailing Standards for Live
Animals
AGENCY: Postal Service
ACTION: Final rule.
SUMMARY: The Postal Service is revising
Publication 52, Hazardous, Restricted,
and Perishable Mail (Pub 52), to
improve package integrity and container
identification for live animal shipments
during processing and handling.
DATES: Effective: October 19, 2026.
FOR FURTHER INFORMATION CONTACT :
Drew Mitchum, (202) 268–7351, or
Jennifer Cox, (202) 268–2108.
SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: The Postal
Service hereby amends Publication 52,
Hazardous, Restricted, and Perishable
Mail (Pub 52 or Publication 52), with
the provisions set forth herein. While
not codified in title 39 of the Code of
Federal Regulations (CFR), Publication
52 is a regulation of the Postal Service,
and changes to it may be published in
the Federal Register. 39 CFR 211.2(a)(2).
Moreover, Publication 52 is
incorporated by reference into Mailing
Standards of the United States Postal
Service, Domestic Mail Manual (DMM)
section 601.8.1, which is incorporated
by reference, in turn, into the Code of
Federal Regulations. 39 CFR 111.1 and
111.3. Publication 52 is publicly
available, in a read-only format, via the
Postal Explorer® website at https://
pe.usps.com. In addition, links to Postal
Explorer are provided on the landing
page of USPS.com, the Postal Service’s
primary customer-facing website, and
on Postal Pro, an online informational
source available to postal customers.
Summary of New Measures
On August 6, 2026, the Postal Service
published a notice of proposed
rulemaking (91 FR 50741) requiring
mailers to individually address and
number live animal shipments
composed of multiple containers bound
together for shipment, recommended
mailers write the recipient’s name and
address directly on the packaging of
adult bird shipments and prohibited the
reuse of fiberboard containers for all live
animal shipments.
Response to Comments
In response to the proposed rule, the
Postal Service received 32 formal
responses to the proposed changes to
Pub 52. The comments received are as
follows:
Comment: One commenter suggested
that USPS establish stronger protection
against temperature extremes, minimize
transportation and delays, require clear
emergency procedures to protect animal
welfare, require appropriate employee
training, review which animals are
appropriate for shipment at all and
consider prohibiting live animal
shipments when safe transportation
can’t be assured.
Response: The Postal Service
appreciates this feedback. Currently,
embargo notices are issued for live
animal shipments when there are travel
interruptions due to weather related
events or holiday transportation
schedules are minimized. To the extent
possible, the Postal Service limits live
animal exposure to temperature
extremes while in transit and ensures all
animals are safely shielded from the
elements while awaiting transport.
Alternatively, the mailer must also
ensure that the live animal shipments
are protected against extreme
temperatures by properly packaging
them and considering both originating
and destinating weather conditions
when shipping, especially during
summer and winter seasons. The postal
service has been actively creating and
providing additional education related
to emergency procedures and proper
handling practices for live animals.
Comment: Several commenters
requested that the name and address
requirement be extended to all lives
shipments not just adult birds, urged
USPS to modify its delivery time period
to 48 hours for day-old poultry, require
a delivery by date on the container for
day-old poultry; and employ specialized
tracking and data collection on lives
perished to better understand the root
causes of documented issues, urges
climate-controlled transportation
modes, requests that USPS issue
moratoriums on the acceptance of lives
as it deems necessary.
Response: The addressing
requirement may be considered for all
live animal shipments in a future
regulatory update. The Postal Service
believes the 48-hour delivery time is not
necessary as the conditions of
acceptance are that the day-old poultry
must not be more than 24 hours old, can
be delivered within 72 hours of
hatching and the hatching date and hour
are required to be indicated on the
shipment. The Postal Service utilizes
tracking data to identify live animal
shipments and monitors data related to
incidents to determine root causes for
documented issues when shipments are
accurately identified by the mailer
through application of the pertinent
USPS extra service codes within their
electronic shipping files. Climate-
controlled transportation for all modes
is not currently feasible, but to the
extent possible, the Postal Service limits
live animal exposure to temperature
extremes while in transit and ensures
they are shielded from the elements
while awaiting transport. Alternatively,
the mailer must also ensure that the live
animal shipments are protected against
extreme temperatures by properly
packaging them and considering both
originating and destinating weather
conditions when shipping, especially
during summer and winter seasons. As
mentioned in the previous comment
response, the Postal Service does
employ embargoes for live animal
shipments when issues arise.
Comment: Several commenters
suggested that USPS require advanced
reservations and confirm transportation
capacity.
Response: The Postal Service is in
contact with major animal shippers and
is provided advance notice of large
shipments of live animals.
Comment: One commenter suggested
that USPS includes specific test
standards for all lives outer packaging
and suggests an alternative to the
address and phone number requirement
of a barcode instead and to require each
container in a multi-container shipment
to have its own address label.
Response: The Postal Service
appreciates the feedback and will

522 Packaging and Markings
* * * * *
522.1 Container Construction
[Revise section to read as follows:]
Containers used to mail perishable
matter must be constructed to protect
and securely contain the contents.
Shipping containers for mailable live
animals must, at a minimum, be made
of 275-pound test, double-wall,
corrugated, weather-resistant fiberboard
(W5c) or equivalent. When multiple
containers are strapped together, a
single shipping label should be applied
but each container must have its own
address label. Additionally, the
containers must be numbered
sequentially (e.g., ‘‘1 of 5’’, ‘‘2 of 5,’’
etc.) to help ensure proper handling if
they become separated during transit.
USPS-produced packaging, including
Flat Rate containers, is not eligible for
use. Additional container requirements
apply to mailable adult birds (refer to
526.4).
