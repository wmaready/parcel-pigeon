AGENCY: Postal Service.
ACTION: Final rule.
SUMMARY: The Postal Service is
amending Mailing Standards of the
United States Postal Service, Domestic
Mail Manual (DMM®), to reflect changes
to prices and certain mailing standards
changes scheduled for competitive
products.
DATES: Effective Date: January 17, 2027.
FOR FURTHER INFORMATION CONTACT:
Catherine Knox (202) 268–5636, Heather
Lewis, (703) 659–3812, or Garry
Rodriguez at (202) 268–7281.
SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: This final
rule describes new prices and product
features for competitive products, by
class of mail, established by the
Governors of the United States Postal
Service®. New prices are available
under Docket Number CP2027–1 on the
Postal Regulatory Commission (PRC)
website at http://www.prc.gov, and on
the Postal Explorer® website at http://
pe.usps.com.
The Postal Service will revise Mailing
Standards of the United States Postal
Service, Domestic Mail Manual (DMM),
to reflect changes to certain prices and
mailing standards for the following
competitive products:
• Priority Mail Express®.
• Priority Mail®.
• USPS Ground Advantage®.
• Parcel Select®.
• Extra Services.
• Mailer Services.
• Recipient Services.
• Other.
Competitive product prices and
changes are identified by product as
follows:
Priority Mail Express
Prices
Overall, Priority Mail Express prices
will increase 5.9 percent. Priority Mail
Express will continue to offer zone and
Flat Rate pricing.
Retail prices will increase an average
of 5.9 percent. The Flat Rate Envelope
price will increase to $34.90, the Legal
Flat Rate Envelope will increase to
$35.20, and the Padded Flat Rate
Envelope will increase to $35.85.
Commercial Base prices will increase
an average of 5.9 percent.

Premium Forwarding Service Redesign
The Postal Service is redesigning
Premium Forwarding Service (PFS)
under a multi-phase plan. In Phase 1,
the Postal Service is redesigning PFS as
follows:
a. PFS Residential will be revised as
follows:
1. The online enrollment fee is
revised to be paid annually in full.
2. The Postal Service is adding
Priority Mail Express as an option for
customer reshipments of mail.
b. PFS Commercial will be revised as
follows:
1. The shipment frequency is changed
to daily, weekly, and monthly.
2. The Postal Service is implementing
a PFS Sack Rehandling Fee, in addition
to postage for shipments sent in sacks.
c. The Postal Service is revising PFS
Residential and PFS Commercial to add
an opt-in automatic renewal feature.
d. The Postal Service is simplifying
the hazardous materials text under PFS
Residential and PFS Local that prohibits
their inclusion in shipments and is
adding this text prohibiting hazardous
materials in PFS Commercial shipments
for consistency.

Dimension Noncompliance Fee
Renamed and Revised
On July 12, 2026, the Postal Service
implemented the first phase of the
Dimension Noncompliance fee requiring
mailers to accurately report all parcel
dimensions in a manifest. The Postal
Service is implementing the second
phase of this initiative. The second
phase will consist of the following:
a. The Postal Service is removing
accuracy as an element of the fee.
b. The Postal Service is adding weight
as an element of the fee.
c. As a result of the addition of
weight, the Postal Service is renaming
the fee Dimension and Weight
Noncompliance Fee.
d. The Postal Service is commencing
to charge the Dimension and Weight
Noncompliance Fee for omitting all
dimensions, weight, or ESC, in the
manifest.
Fuel Surcharge
The Postal Service is implementing a
Fuel Surcharge. The Fuel Surcharge will
be assessed on all retail and commercial
priced Priority Mail Express, Priority
Mail, and USPS Ground Advantage
pieces, including USPS Returns, and
Parcel Select pieces (except for USPS
Connect Local).
In its initial phase, the Fuel Surcharge
will be set at 11 percent across
applicable products and must be
calculated as a percentage of the base
postage price of each product. In the
future, the Fuel Surcharge is intended to
be dynamic and self-adjusting based on
relevant fuel prices. The data used to
create the Fuel Surcharge will be
leveraged from the U.S. Energy
Information Administration website
available at eia.gov. The ground
products (USPS Ground Advantage and
Parcel Select) surcharge will be based
on National U.S. Average On-Highway
Diesel Fuel (EPD2D) and the air
products (Priority Mail Express and
Priority Mail) surcharge will be based
on U.S. Gulf Coast Kerosene-type Jet
Fuel prices (EPJK). As such, the
structure for a dynamic Fuel Surcharge
is being implemented now with a fixed
11 percent surcharge across all fuel
prices, with the intent that such
percentages will likely be adjusted and
varied in future proceedings.
The surcharge will be posted on the
Postal Explorer website at pe.usps.com,

Shipping Label Noncompliance Fee
The Postal Service is implementing a
Shipping Label Noncompliance Fee of
$5.00. A mailpiece will be considered
noncompliant when mail processing
equipment detects a label with an
incorrect Intelligent Mail® package
barcode (IMpb®) symbology or incorrect
barcode construct (content within the
barcode).
This fee will be assessed on each
noncompliant domestic commercial
Priority Mail Express, Priority Mail,
USPS Ground Advantage, and Parcel
Select mailpiece, including USPS
Returns (Priority Mail Express Return,
Priority Mail Return, and USPS Ground
Advantage Return).
Insufficient Postage (Shortpaid) and
Overpaid Postage Standards for USPS
Ship, APV, and PC Postage
The Postal Service is establishing
standards for pieces that have
insufficient postage (shortpaid) and for
the refund of postage on overpaid
pieces, manifested through USPS Ship,
captured through the Automated
Package Verification (APV) program, or
with postage paid using PC Postage. The
standards set forth below for insufficient
postage and overpaid pieces will apply
to commercial Priority Mail Express,
Priority Mail, USPS Ground Advantage
and Parcel Select.
The Postal Service is revising DMM
section 604.8.0, Insufficient or Omitted
Postage, to add new subsection 8.1.12 to
provide the standards for the collection
of postage due on pieces detected by
USPS Ship or the APV program with
insufficient postage. Mailers will be
liable for the total amount of postage
due. The Postal Service is implementing
a ‘‘Repricing Fee’’ of 20 percent of the
postage due on each piece detected by
USPS Ship and the APV program with
insufficient postage. The total postage
due (insufficient postage amount plus
repricing fee) will be deducted from the
mailer’s account.
Additionally, the Postal Service is
revising DMM section 604.9.0,
Exchanges and Refunds, to add a new
subsection 9.7 to provide the standards
for the refund of postage on eligible
pieces mailed through USPS Ship or
with PC Postage. Mailers will be
required to request a refund for overpaid
pieces. Refund requests for overpaid
pieces will be subject to a $5.00
threshold (only refund requests for more
than $5.00 will be eligible for a refund).
The Postal Service is implementing a
‘‘Repricing Fee’’ of 20 percent on each
piece that is eligible for a refund.
Mailers will be required to apply for a
refund under DMM 9.2.5, Applying for
Refund, within the time limits provided
in DMM Exhibit 604.9.2.1, Postage and
Fees Refunds, and receive payment as
provided under DMM 9.2.6, Refund
Payment.
Overweight and Oversize Items Fee
The overweight and oversize items fee
will increase 100 percent, from $200 to
$400.

I. Executive Summary
A. What action is the EPA taking?
The Environmental Protection Agency
(EPA) is finalizing a limited approval
and limited disapproval of three
permitting rules submitted by the San
Joaquin Valley Unified Air Pollution
Control District (SJVUAPCD or
‘‘District’’) as a revision to the California
SIP. The submitted rules address the
District’s New Source Review (NSR) air
permitting program rules for new and
modified sources of air pollution
pursuant to CAA section 110(a)(2)(C)
and part D of title I. This action will
update the California SIP with rules that
the District has revised to address
deficiencies identified in a previous
limited disapproval action and to
incorporate other revisions related to
Nonattainment New Source Review
(NNSR) requirements. We proposed this
action on January 21, 2025, and held a
30-day public comment period.
B. What is the legal authority and what
are the requirements?
CAA sections 110(k)(3) and 301(a)
provide the EPA’s statutory authority for
finalizing a limited approval and
limited disapproval of Rules 1020, 2020,
and 2201. We have evaluated the rule
revisions for compliance with CAA
sections 172(c)(5), 173, 182(e), and
189(b), which establish the
requirements for stationary source
preconstruction permitting programs,
including those specifically applicable
for an Extreme ozone nonattainment
area and Serious PM2.5 nonattainment
area, as well as the Federal regulations
applicable to stationary source
permitting at 40 CFR 51.160 through
51.165. Additionally, the EPA reviewed
the rules for consistency with other
general CAA requirements for SIP
submittals, including requirements at
CAA section 110(a)(2)(A) regarding rule
enforceability and requirements at CAA
sections 110(l) and 193 for SIP
revisions. We have also considered
whether the rules meet the Federal
visibility requirements related to State
NNSR programs as described in 40 CFR
51.307.
